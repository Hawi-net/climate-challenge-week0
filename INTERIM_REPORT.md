Interim Report: African Climate Trends AnalysisExecutive Summary
This report summarizes the progress made on the climate data analysis for COP32. The project involves processing and analyzing NASA POWER climate data for Ethiopia, Kenya, Nigeria, Sudan, and Tanzania.Task 1: Environment & Version ControlSetup: A dedicated virtual environment was created to ensure reproducible results across all five country analyses.Git Strategy: I implemented a branch-per-country workflow (eda-ethiopia, eda-kenya, etc.) to maintain clean version history and organized documentation.Security: A .gitignore file was utilized to keep raw and processed CSV data out of the public repository, following data privacy best practices.Task 2: Data Profiling & Cleaning ApproachMy approach to preparing the dataset for analysis included several key technical steps:Handling NASA Headers: I skipped the metadata lines to correctly read the feature columns.Sentinel Replacement: NASA's -999 values were identified as missing data and replaced with NaN to prevent statistical skewing.Date Engineering: I converted Julian dates (Year + Day of Year) into standard calendar formats for better seasonal tracking.Outlier Strategy: I used Z-score Analysis to find extreme values. I chose to retain outliers (values with $|Z| > 3$) because extreme rainfall and heatwaves are vital for climate change reporting.Data Export: All cleaned data was successfully exported to a local data/ directory for use in modeling.Preliminary FindingsSeasonal Shifts: Distinct rainy seasons were identified across East and West Africa.Correlations: A high correlation between wind speed averages and maximums ($r \approx 0.95$) and a strong negative correlation between heat and humidity ($r \approx -0.80$) were found consistently across all countries.
